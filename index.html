Key Insights · GEO Modal

  • Spanish-speaking regions led routing with ~7,250 combined loads — spread across Venezuela, Bolivia, El Salvador, Dominican Republic, Uruguay, Panama, Paraguay and more, reflecting the broad geographic reach of Spanish in Latin America.
  • Top 5 target locales (es, kr, fr, de, br) account for ~21,800 of 26,169 total loads — 83% of routing is concentrated in 5 destination locales out of 17.
  • French routing included substantial volumes from Morocco (MA: 785 combined) and Algeria (DZ: 499 combined) — French-speaking Africa is a meaningful audience segment.
  • Reverse routing (kr→us: 1,562 combined) is the largest non-US-sourced flow, suggesting Korean-locale visitors on the US site being routed back — a potential UX friction point worth investigating.
  • The long tail is wide: 88 distinct source→target routes on Apr 27 alone, reaching countries from Mongolia (MO) to Ireland (IE), Israel (IL), New Zealand (NZ), and Mexico (MX).
  • "Visitor Country" comes from Adobe Analytics' geo-IP lookup on the user's IP — independent of the locale they're routed to. This explains entries like us→es · ve (visitor in Venezuela on US site, routed to Spanish locale).

Key Insights · Language Banner

  • es→us is the #1 pair with 6,724 combined events — Spanish-speaking users hitting the US site and being shown a banner to switch.
  • Top 5 pairs (es→us, us→es, br→us, id_id→us, de→us) account for 25,612 of 42,341 total banner events (60%).
  • "Close" interactions (users dismissing the banner without switching) totalled 452 — about 1.1% of all banner events, indicating most users engage with the destination suggestion rather than dismissing it.
  • Top 10 locale pairs accounted for 34,646 of 42,341 total (82%), showing the banner is highly concentrated in a small set of high-volume routing pairs.
  • Long-tail Continue clicks (es|Continue, us|Continue, jp|Continue, etc.) totalled 121 — users actively confirming their language choice rather than auto-switching.
  • The bidirectional flow is notable: us→es (5,179) is the second-largest pair, complementing es→us (6,724). The two-way switching suggests users are exploring both locales before settling.

Key Insights · Market Selector

  • Dismissed (1,016) outnumbered Opened (811) — a 56/44 split — suggesting users notice the selector but choose not to engage with it in the majority of cases.
  • The US homepage drove 63% of all market-selector interactions (1,152 of 1,827), confirming it as the primary discovery surface for the geo locator feature.
  • Total interactions (1,827) are small relative to GEO Modal loads (26,169) — most users see the routing modal but only ~7% take the explicit action of opening or dismissing the market selector.
  • The opened-to-dismissed ratio is consistent across the active period — stable user behaviour rather than a launch-day novelty effect.
